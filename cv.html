Who am I?

I'm a computer vision researcher and a hobbyist tinkerer, who likes to figure out how things work, and use that knowledge to build something new. My research has focused on how state-of-the-art computer vision systems perform outside the lab, where input data is noisy and full of artefacts. Trying to figure out what signal we can capture from the data, and what changes could help isolate that signal, has led me to work on long-term deployments of vision systems in the wild, and on evaluation methods that measure how well a system aligns with human values. I care more about whether a system working as intended when conditions change than about a small gain on a benchmark.

Postdoc · Aalborg University & CAISA

My postdoc work continues my interest on how to measure whether a system is doing the right thing when faced with complex real-world scenarios. Part of that work has been on specific usecases related to implementation of Responsible AI, in sensative contexts, where people are at risk of being harmed or are in vulnurable postions where privacy and dignity are paramount. Another part of my work involves working on how to operationalize the AI Act for both industry and academia, Primarily questioning how we can translate ethical, societal and legal requirements into computable metrics. While retaining alignment with the human-centric values that many Responsible AI frameworks claim to support.

At the sametime i get to take part of my schedule to work on designing and running vision-based data-collection setups for long-term deployments. I corrently do this as a part of a marine vision workpackage in the EU-funded LOTUS-PTO project, where I designed and build underwater vision setups that are exposed to the open ocean for prolonged periods, to asses the growth patterns of bio-fouling.

Projects

  • Computing Responsible AI. The AI Act asks that systems "achieve an appropriate level of accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity...". However, when they are translated into measurable targets, important nuances can be lost. I work on trying to bridge that gap, while identifying which components get lost in translation. The key is to identify which normative principles survive being made computable, and which quietly change meaning or measure something else.
    In addition to computer-vision grounded research,I organize workshops and give talks with industry and academia, addressing the challenges of translating normative principles and operationalizing them as measurable targets. More crucially, trying to underline that adhering to a comparative benchmark does not mean that the system is actually measuring the right thing, and that a system can be compliant with the AI Act while still being misaligned with human values.
  • REPAI (Responsible AI for Value Creation), funded by the Poul Due Jensen Foundation (with KU and AU). I built and trained the models for real-world cases, defined how they were evaluated, and ran the work with the domain experts. I continued my PhD work on object recognition in low-resolution thermal data, and worked on eldercare-visit summaries for Aalborg Municipality's care staff. The code, models and results from the harbour-front case were delivered to the harbour-safety authorities, along with the documentation and the public dataset; the eldercare track ran through workshops with care professionals.
  • LOTUS-PTO (Laser-Textured Surfaces for Wave and Tidal Power Take-Off), an EU consortium led by RISE (2025-2028). I designed and built the underwater imaging rig end to end: camera and optics, the RP2350 firmware and HTTP API controlling the illumination and the lens wiper, the capture and scheduling software, the processing tools and the housing. A first rig then ran a full 135-day season without any interruption, with the capture and storage pipeline holding up throughout.
  • JAMBO, a study of the seabed in Jammerbugten, Nordjylland, looking at the impact of beam-trawl operations and classifying existing habitats. An interdisciplinary study, together with a team of marine biologists, where I ran the ROV surveys, built the annotation protocol and the tooling behind it, briefed the six annotators, and did the data collection behind the paper. The result is a 3,290-image dataset in which every image carries six independent labels, built so that annotator disagreement can be measured rather than assumed away.

PhD Fellowship · Aalborg University

The focus of my PhD, was to figure out what happends when object recognition algorithms leave the lab, and get exposed to the real-world under unconstrained conditions. Figuring out how the performance of these systems could be measured in a way that was meaningful outside the lab, and whether the models could be adapted to hold up under continuous data drift.

Initially i contributed to the LTD dataset with object-detection annotation, curation and evaluation. Then i organized the Seasons in Drift challenge, designed the evaluation and ran the evaluation infrastructure. On the method side I developed weather inference so that thermal detection could be conditioned on the meteorological conditions in the observed environment. The co-authored comparison and survey work came out of the same period.

Notable Outputs

  • Seasons in Drift / LTD (NeurIPS 2021 Datasets & Benchmarks): the annotation and curation pipeline behind 298 hours of thermal video over eight months, later extended to 1,689 clips across 188 days with 1.07M annotated frames and 6.87M boxes, plus the weather metadata that lets a change in performance be explained.
  • ChaLearn LAP Seasons in Drift Challenge (ECCV 2022 Workshops): I organised a challenge on the LTD dataset, designed to assess how well object detection models hold up under continous data drift, and designed the evaluation protocol to highlight how performance drifts across seasons and correlated performance with weather conditions.
  • Who Cares about the Weather? (Applied Sciences, 2023): I developed the weather-inference method for weather-aware thermal detection and authored the paper. Aiming to embed a projected representation of the weather conditions both directly (forcing the model to adapt) and indirectly (providing a condition vector with a learnable weighting scheme) to improve detection performance in low-resolution thermal data.
  • Video Transformers: A Survey (IEEE TPAMI, 2023): Co-authored the first survey on transformer architectures when applied to high-dimensional video data, where both strong temporal and spatial attention mechanisms are discussed. Moreover the different approaches to address them and their associated computational complexities are compared compared.
  • Joint Semanstic Segmentation and Super-Resolution (LNCS CAIP, 2023 and WACW-W, 2021): Co-authored two conference papers focused on joint-learning of semantic segmentation and super-resolution, where the goal is to improve the performance of super-resolution in contexts where there exists no high-resolution groundtruth, and further leverage that to improve semantic understanding.

Research Assistant · Aalborg University

In the Autonomous Sewer Inspection Robot (ASIR) project, I worked on buildingt a prototype sensor package that could operate on a small robot in a sewer pipe for extended periods of time. The challenge was to design a system that could acquire, process and store data in real time, while being constrained by the limited power and space available on the robot. I designed the prototype vision-data acquisition setup from the camera selection, the mounting and enclosure, the capture and storage software, and the on-board processing integration and tested it in the lab until it could acquire, process and store footage in real time.

While joining the ASIR project i also co-authored two papers from projects i did during the master's degree. First being about multi-modal heartbeat-rate estimation from rgb and thermal video, and second being about affect-based trust estimation in human-robot interaction.

Notable Outputs

  • Affect-Based Trust Estimation (IEEE HRI, 2020): Co-authored a paper on affect-based trust estimation in human-robot interaction, where participants would interact with a robotic arm displaying different movement behavior and interaction patterns while their affective state was measured to estimate their physiological response to their percieved trust in the robot.
  • Multi-Modal Heartbeat Estimation (ICMV, 2019): Co-authored a paper on heartbeat-rate estimation from multi-modal video, where we fused visual and motion-based information. The method showed that a combination of motion and visual based information beats out unimodal approaches and that decomposing the information into different sampling regimes could result in a more robust estimation of the heartbeat rate.
  • 3D Sensors for Sewer Inspection (Sensors, 2021): Co-authored the quantitative comparison of depth sensing for pipe inspection, where the robustness of compact 3D sensors were evaluated in hostile conditions where there is limited visibility, reflective surfaces, dynamic lighting, contigous uniform feature-poor surfaces.
